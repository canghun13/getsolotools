Late Fee Laws for Freelancers & Small Businesses in Hawaii

November 23, 2026 · 7 min read

Hawaii is friendlier to freelancers than its island economy might suggest: a 10% default rate when your contract is silent, no cap on the rate a business client agrees to in writing, and a statute that makes the losing side in a contract case pay attorney fees of up to 25% of the judgment. The catches are a low $5,000 small claims ceiling and a 12% cap if your client is a consumer. Here's what Hawaii freelancers need to know.

No Rate in the Contract? Hawaii Applies 10%

Under HRS § 478-2, when interest is owed and no rate was agreed, Hawaii applies 10% a year — one of the higher statutory defaults in the country. That makes even a contract with no late fee clause worth something on an overdue invoice. Judgments also carry 10% a year (§ 478-3).

Business Clients vs. Consumers: The 12% Line

HRS § 478-4 splits clients into two groups. For consumer credit transactions (personal, family or household purposes) and home business loans, a written contract can't exceed 1% a month — 12% a year simple interest. For non-consumer transactions — which covers a freelancer billing a company for business services — the parties may stipulate in writing any rate not otherwise prohibited by law. The penalty for going over the limit is steep: the creditor recovers only principal (§ 478-5), and knowingly charging excessive interest is a crime (§ 478-6).

Practical rule: for a business client, 1.5% a month in a signed contract is fine. For an individual hiring you for a personal project — a wedding photographer's client, a homeowner's designer — stay at or under 1% a month.

Attorney Fees of Up to 25% in Contract Cases

Hawaii's strongest tool for freelancers is HRS § 607-14. In any action "in the nature of assumpsit" — which includes a suit to collect on an unpaid contract — the court awards reasonable attorney fees to the prevailing party, taxed against the loser, capped at 25% of the judgment. If your contract sets a lower percentage, the award is limited to that figure. The rule cuts both ways: if the client wins, the fee is calculated on the amount you sued for. Mention it in a final demand letter only when your invoice is solid.

Small Claims: $5,000, and No Appeal

Hawaii's Small Claims Division of District Court hears money claims up to $5,000, with a $35 filing fee and claims filed in person at the District Court's Legal Documents Branch. Parties may bring a lawyer, but attorney fees generally aren't awarded in small claims, and there is no right to appeal a small claims decision. For a larger invoice, the regular District Court civil docket — where § 607-14 fees come into play — is the better route.

Six Years to Sue

Hawaii allows 6 years to sue on a contract (HRS § 657-1), so an unpaid invoice doesn't have to be chased in a panic — but the evidence is stronger while emails and delivery records are fresh.

Setting Up Enforceable Late Fees in Hawaii

  • Identify the client as a business in the contract — it's what puts the rate outside the 12% consumer cap in § 478-4.
  • Write 1.5% a month for business clients, 1% for consumers, and state it on every invoice.
  • Add an attorney fee clause at 25% if you want the full § 607-14 cap; a lower contract percentage limits the award.
  • Keep the invoice and delivery trail — small claims decisions can't be appealed, so the first hearing is the only one.

Collecting in Hawaii

  • Small Claims Division: up to $5,000, $35 filing fee, no appeal
  • District Court civil docket: larger claims, with attorney fees up to 25% of the judgment (HRS § 607-14)
  • Default interest with no agreed rate: 10% a year (§ 478-2); judgments 10% (§ 478-3)
  • Statute of limitations: 6 years on a contract (§ 657-1)

Frequently Asked Questions

What late fee rate can I charge in Hawaii? +

For a business (non-consumer) client, any rate the client agrees to in writing that isn't otherwise prohibited by law (HRS § 478-4) — 1.5% per month is common. For consumer clients, the limit is 1% per month (12% a year). With no agreed rate, 10% a year applies (§ 478-2).

Can I recover attorney fees for an unpaid invoice in Hawaii? +

Yes, in most contract collection suits. HRS § 607-14 awards the prevailing party reasonable attorney fees in assumpsit actions, capped at 25% of the judgment (or a lower percentage if your contract sets one). Fees generally aren't awarded in small claims.

What is the small claims limit in Hawaii? +

$5,000 for money claims in the Small Claims Division of District Court, with a $35 filing fee. There is no right to appeal a small claims decision.

How long do I have to sue over an unpaid invoice in Hawaii? +

Six years on a contract claim (HRS § 657-1).
