Late Fee Laws for Freelancers & Small Businesses in Idaho

October 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Idaho is quietly one of the best states in this series to send an invoice from. Its default interest rate is 12% a year — higher than any neighbor — there's no general cap on a rate you write into a contract, and a fee-shifting statute can make a client who refuses to pay cover your lawyer. Here's what Idaho freelancers and small businesses need to know.

No Late Fee Clause? Idaho Still Gives You 12% a Year

Under Idaho Code § 28-22-104, interest is allowed at 12% per annum on money due by express contract and on money after it becomes due. That's one of the most generous statutory default rates anywhere — Utah and Arizona use 10%, Oregon 9%. If your contract says nothing about late fees, an overdue invoice still carries 12% a year from the due date.

The open-account catch. The same statute treats open accounts — ongoing work billed as you go, without a fixed contract for the job — differently: interest runs only after three months from the date of the last item. A signed contract or accepted proposal with a due date avoids the wait. One more reason to get the engagement in writing.

Is There a Cap on a Written Late Fee in Idaho?

Idaho has no general statutory ceiling on an interest rate agreed in writing. So the national-standard 1.5% a month (18% a year) is on solid ground for a written freelance contract — and that's what the calculator above defaults to.

That nearly changed. House Bill 649 (2026) would have capped agreed rates at the greater of 30% or 10 points above prime. It cleared committee in early March, was sent back to committee on March 25, 2026, and did not become law. Even if a cap like that returns, it would sit far above any normal invoice late fee. The practical limit is the same as everywhere: a charge large enough to look like punishment rather than compensation — particularly one stated per day — can be struck as a penalty. Idaho Can Make the Client Pay Your Attorney

This is the Idaho rule that changes the economics of collecting. Under Idaho Code § 12-120(3), the prevailing party in a civil action on an open account, an account stated, a contract relating to the purchase or sale of services, or any commercial transaction is awarded reasonable attorney's fees. "Commercial transaction" covers everything except personal or household purchases — so an invoice to a business client is squarely in.

  • It cuts both ways. If the client wins, they recover their fees from you. File when your contract, invoices and delivery records are clean.
  • Smaller claims have a second route. § 12-120(1) awards fees on claims of $35,000 or less if you made a written demand at least 10 days before filing — but not if the client had already offered at least 95% of what the court awards.
  • Say so in your demand letter. A client who realizes that refusing to pay could mean paying two lawyers often settles before anyone files.

Idaho Small Claims: $5,000, No Lawyers

Small claims in Idaho is a department of the magistrate division of district court, with a ceiling of $5,000 (Idaho Code § 1-2301). Lawyers can't represent either side, so it's built for you to file yourself. Above $5,000 you'd file a regular civil case — which is where § 12-120 makes hiring a lawyer far easier to justify. Setting Up Enforceable Late Fees in Idaho

  • Write the rate into the contract — 1.5% a month is standard and uncapped in Idaho.
  • Use a signed contract or accepted proposal with a due date, so interest isn't delayed by the open-account rule.
  • Keep a clean paper trail. Under § 12-120 the winner's lawyer gets paid — make sure that's you.
  • Send a written demand at least 10 days before filing on any claim of $35,000 or less.

Collecting in Idaho

  • Small claims (magistrate division): up to $5,000; attorneys not permitted (§ 1-2301)
  • Attorney's fees to the prevailing party on services contracts and commercial transactions (§ 12-120(3)); on claims of $35,000 or less after a 10-day written demand (§ 12-120(1))
  • Default interest: 12% a year on money due by contract (§ 28-22-104); on open accounts, after three months from the last item
  • Statute of limitations: 5 years for written contracts (§ 5-216), 4 years for oral contracts (§ 5-217)

What interest can I charge in Idaho if my contract doesn't mention a late fee? +

12% a year. Idaho Code § 28-22-104 allows interest at 12% per annum on money due by express contract and on money after it becomes due. That is one of the highest statutory default rates in the country. One catch: on an open account (ongoing work billed without a fixed contract), interest runs only after three months from the date of the last item.

Is there a cap on late fees in Idaho? +

Idaho has no general statutory ceiling on a rate agreed in a written contract, so the common 1.5% a month is on solid ground. A 2026 bill (House Bill 649) proposed capping agreed rates at the greater of 30% or prime plus 10 points, but it was sent back to committee in March 2026 and did not become law. A very aggressive fee can still fail as an unenforceable penalty.

Can I recover attorney's fees for an unpaid invoice in Idaho? +

Often, yes. Idaho Code § 12-120(3) awards reasonable attorney's fees to the prevailing party in a civil action on an open account, an account stated, a contract for services, or any commercial transaction, meaning any transaction that is not for personal or household purposes. It works both ways: if the client wins, the client gets fees, so file only when your paperwork is solid. Section 12-120(1) separately allows fees on claims of $35,000 or less if you made a written demand at least 10 days before suing.

What is the small claims limit in Idaho? +

$5,000, heard by a magistrate in the small claims department of the district court (Idaho Code § 1-2301). Attorneys may not represent parties in Idaho small claims. Statute of limitations is 5 years for a written contract (§ 5-216) and 4 years for an oral one (§ 5-217).
