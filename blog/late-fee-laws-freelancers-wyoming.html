Late Fee Laws for Freelancers & Small Businesses in Wyoming

November 23, 2026 · 7 min read

Wyoming keeps collection cheap and slow-moving in the freelancer's favor: a $10 small claims filing fee, ten years to sue on a written contract, and 10% interest once you have a judgment. Its 7% default rate is modest, so the rate in your contract does most of the work. Here's what Wyoming freelancers need to know.

The 7% Default Rate

Under W.S. § 40-14-106(e), when there's no agreement or other law setting a different rate, interest is 7% a year. That's a modest fallback, so a written rate matters more in Wyoming than in high-default states like New Mexico or Alaska.

Agreed Rates: Consumer Rules vs. Business Clients

Wyoming's interest limits sit in its Uniform Consumer Credit Code, which regulates credit extended to consumers. For a freelancer billing a business client, the rate agreed in the contract generally controls, and 1% to 1.5% a month is common. If your client is an individual buying personal or household services on credit, keep the rate modest and clearly disclosed, since consumer-credit rules may apply. In either case, a rate stated per day can be attacked as a penalty — see is a daily late fee legal on an invoice.

Small Claims: $6,000 for a $10 Filing Fee

Wyoming's circuit courts hear small claims up to $6,000; with a larger claim you either waive the excess or file in another court. The filing fee is $10, plus the sheriff's or process server's fee for each defendant. You don't need a lawyer, and a judge — not a jury — hears the case.

Ten Years on a Written Contract

Wyoming allows 10 years to sue on a written contract and 8 years on an oral one (W.S. § 1-3-105) — among the longest windows in the country. Once you win, the judgment carries 10% a year until paid (W.S. § 1-16-102).

Setting Up Enforceable Late Fees in Wyoming

  • Write the rate into the contract — without it you're limited to 7% a year.
  • For business clients, 1% to 1.5% a month is common; for individual consumers, keep it modest and disclosed.
  • Put the rate and due date on every invoice.
  • Use small claims for anything up to $6,000 — the $10 fee makes it one of the cheapest court routes in the region.

Collecting in Wyoming

  • Small claims (circuit court): up to $6,000, $10 filing fee plus service fees
  • Default interest with no agreed rate: 7% a year (W.S. § 40-14-106(e))
  • Judgment interest: 10% a year (W.S. § 1-16-102)
  • Statute of limitations: 10 years written, 8 years oral (W.S. § 1-3-105)

Frequently Asked Questions

What interest applies in Wyoming if my contract doesn't state a rate? +

7% a year under W.S. § 40-14-106(e). Judgments carry 10% a year (W.S. § 1-16-102).

What is the small claims limit in Wyoming? +

$6,000 in circuit court, with a $10 filing fee plus service fees for each defendant. A judge hears the case, and you don't need a lawyer.

How long do I have to sue over an unpaid invoice in Wyoming? +

Ten years on a written contract and eight years on an oral one (W.S. § 1-3-105).
