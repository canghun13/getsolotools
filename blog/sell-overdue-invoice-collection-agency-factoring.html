Can You Sell an Overdue Invoice? Factoring vs. Collection Agency vs. Debt Buyer

November 2, 2026 · 8 min read

When an invoice goes seriously overdue, there's a moment where chasing it yourself stops feeling worth it and the question changes: can I just sell this, or hand it to someone else? You can — but the three options people mean (factoring, a collection agency, or selling the debt outright) work very differently, cost very different amounts, and two of them may not be available at all once an invoice is old. This page puts them side by side with the numbers.

Option 1: Factoring — Mostly Off the Table Once an Invoice Is Late

Invoice factoring means selling an unpaid invoice to a finance company for an advance — typically 70% to 90% of face value up front — with the rest, minus a discount fee commonly in the 2% to 5% range, paid when your client settles. Some factors do spot factoring of a single invoice, though most prefer to take all or most of a business's invoices.

The problem is timing. Factoring is priced on your client's likelihood of paying on schedule, and an invoice that's already late is a bad signal. Factors commonly cap how overdue an invoice can be — many at 30 days past due — and very few will touch one more than 90 days late. And most factoring is recourse: if your client never pays, you repay the advance. Factoring an invoice you're worried about doesn't move the risk off you.

Where factoring does fit: a freshly issued invoice to a solid client on long terms (net 60, net 90) when you need the cash now. It's a cash-flow tool, not a collection tool.

Option 2: A Collection Agency — You Keep the Claim, They Keep a Cut

Commercial collection agencies usually work on contingency: no fee unless they collect, and a percentage of whatever they do recover. Rates published by agencies themselves run roughly:

Agencies also report that recovery rates fall as an account ages, by around 1% a week, which is why handing a claim over at day 75 is worth more than at day 300. Ask before signing: does the agency only send letters and call, or will it refer to a lawyer and sue? And who pays court costs if it does?

Option 3: Sell the Debt Outright

Debt buyers purchase overdue B2B invoices for cash, then collect them for their own account. One buyer that publishes its pricing reports discounts of 35% to 45% off face value as common, within a wider 25%–75% range — so a well-documented $4,000 invoice might sell for roughly $2,200 to $2,600. What drives the price:

  • Proof — a signed contract or accepted quote, delivery evidence, and your payment demands. A file built from loose emails sells for less.
  • The client's ability to pay — a solvent business that's just slow is worth far more than one that has gone quiet.
  • Age — the price drops as the claim approaches your state's statute of limitations.

Selling is the only option that turns an uncertain claim into certain cash today. The trade-off is that you give up the upside and the leverage.

What You Lose When the Invoice Changes Hands

Once you sell or assign the claim, the buyer stands in your shoes — but not always with the same tools:

  • Small claims may close. Several states keep assigned claims out of small claims court: Nebraska and Kansas exclude them, and Indiana requires an attorney for assigned claims. That pushes the buyer toward costlier court — and is part of why buyers pay less.
  • Your personal leverage goes with it. Some of the strongest remedies attach to you as the freelancer — the double damages under New York's, Illinois's and California's freelance protection acts, for example. Use them in your own demand letter before deciding to sell.
  • The client relationship ends. A collection agency or buyer contacting your client is a clear signal you're done working together.

Before You Sell: Use the Free Leverage First

A demand letter costs nothing, and in several states it changes the client's math sharply:

  • Texas: a claim for services lets you recover attorney's fees if the client doesn't pay within 30 days of presentment — Texas guide.
  • Idaho: the winner of a commercial dispute gets attorney's fees — Idaho guide.
  • Mississippi: a written, itemized 30-day demand on an open account puts attorney's fees on the table — Mississippi guide.
  • New York and Rhode Island: courts add 9% and 12% a year respectively from the due date, even with no late fee clause — New York, Rhode Island.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I sell a single overdue invoice? +

Sometimes, but not usually to a factoring company. Factors buy invoices that are current or only slightly late; many cap past-due invoices at 30 days and very few will take one more than 90 days overdue. An invoice that is already well overdue is typically sold to a debt buyer instead, at a discount that often runs 35% to 45% off face value for a well-documented claim, or placed with a collection agency on contingency.

How much does a collection agency charge to collect a business invoice? +

Commercial collection agencies usually work on contingency, keeping a percentage of what they collect and nothing if they collect nothing. Published agency rates run roughly 35% or more on balances under $3,000, 25% to 35% on $3,000 to $10,000, and 10% to 25% above $10,000, with higher rates for older accounts or when a lawyer has to be involved.

Is it better to sell an unpaid invoice or send it to collections? +

Selling gives certain cash now at a discount and ends your involvement; collections keeps more of each dollar recovered but only pays if the agency actually collects. If the client is solvent and the paperwork is strong, collections or small claims usually nets more. If the client looks shaky or the invoice is old, a certain 55 cents on the dollar can beat an uncertain 70.

What happens to my rights if I sell or assign the invoice? +

The buyer steps into your claim, and some doors close. Several states bar assigned claims from small claims court; Nebraska and Kansas exclude them outright and Indiana requires an attorney for assigned claims. Statutory extras tied to you as the freelancer, such as a freelance protection act's double damages, may not travel with the claim. Use your own leverage first: a demand letter that cites your state's interest or attorney's fee rules costs nothing.

Does factoring work if the client is already late? +

Rarely. Factoring is a financing tool for invoices that are expected to be paid on time; factors price the client's credit, and a late payer is a bad credit signal. Under recourse factoring, the more common kind, you must also repay the advance if the client never pays, so factoring a doubtful invoice does not transfer the risk.
