Late Fee Laws for Freelancers & Small Businesses in Rhode Island

October 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Rhode Island is unusually generous to a freelancer without a late fee clause: if you win a claim for an unpaid invoice, the court clerk adds 12% a year from the day it was due, whether or not your contract mentions a late fee. The flip side is a hard 21% ceiling on interest, with no exemption for business clients. Here's what Rhode Island freelancers and small businesses need to know.

No Late Fee Clause? Rhode Island Adds 12% — Automatically

This is the Rhode Island rule that matters most. Under R.I. Gen. Laws § 9-21-10, in any civil action where damages are awarded, the clerk of the court adds interest at 12% a year from the date the cause of action accrued. For an unpaid invoice, that's normally the day it went overdue. You don't have to ask for it as a separate item, prove a loss, or have a clause in your contract — it's added to the judgment.

  • 12% is high. Most states' no-contract defaults sit between 5% and 10%. Rhode Island's equals 1% a month.
  • It runs from accrual, not from filing. A $6,000 invoice that's a year overdue by the time you win carries about $720 of statutory interest.
  • Your contract rate governs if you have one. The automatic 12% doesn't apply where the contract already provides for interest — so a written rate below 12% would actually cost you.
  • Post-judgment interest is also 12% until the client pays.

The 21% Ceiling — With No Business Exemption

Rhode Island's usury statute, R.I. Gen. Laws § 6-26-2, caps interest at the greater of 21% a year or an alternate rate tied to Treasury bill yields. What makes Rhode Island different is what's missing: many states exempt business-to-business credit from their caps, and Rhode Island has no such general exemption. Treat 21% as the line regardless of who your client is.

In practice: the national-standard 1.5% a month (18% a year) fits comfortably, which is why the calculator above defaults to it. 2% a month (24% a year) does not. And because the automatic 12% beats any written rate below it, there's little reason to write anything under 1% a month.

Whatever rate you choose, state it monthly or annually, not per day — see is a daily late fee legal on an invoice. If you add a flat fee on top of interest, keep it a modest one-time charge; can you charge a flat late fee and interest on the same invoice explains why.

Rhode Island Small Claims: $5,000 Since 2021

The District Court's small claims division hears claims up to $5,000, excluding interest and costs (R.I. Gen. Laws § 10-16-1). The ceiling went up from $2,500 effective July 3, 2021, and some guides still quote the old number. Because interest and costs are excluded from the cap, the automatic 12% sits on top of a full $5,000 claim. Setting Up Enforceable Late Fees in Rhode Island

  • Write 1–1.5% a month, not less. Below 1% a month, the automatic 12% would have served you better.
  • Never above 21% a year, even with a business client — no 2% a month clauses.
  • Put the rate and due date on every invoice, not only in the original agreement.
  • Keep the due date provable. The 12% runs from the date the claim accrued, so a dated invoice with clear terms is what sets the clock.

Collecting in Rhode Island

  • Small claims (District Court): up to $5,000, excluding interest and costs (§ 10-16-1, since July 3, 2021)
  • Prejudgment interest: 12% a year from accrual, added by the clerk where the contract sets no rate (§ 9-21-10)
  • Post-judgment interest: 12% a year (§ 9-21-10)
  • Usury ceiling: greater of 21% a year or the Treasury-linked alternate rate (§ 6-26-2)
  • Statute of limitations: 10 years for most civil actions
