Late Fee Laws for Freelancers & Small Businesses in South Dakota

November 23, 2026 · 7 min read

South Dakota is the easiest state in the country to charge a late fee in. There's no usury limit on a rate agreed in writing, an interest rate printed on your invoice can be enforced up to 18% even without a separate contract clause, and the statutory rate on money after it's due is 15% a year. Here's what South Dakota freelancers need to know.

No Usury Limit on a Written Rate

SDCL § 54-3-1.1 is titled exactly what it does: a rate of interest set by written agreement has no maximum and no usury restriction. That's why South Dakota is home to so many credit card banks — and why a freelancer's written late fee clause can't be attacked as usurious. A rate that functions as a penalty can still be challenged, so keep it in the normal commercial range.

A Rate on Your Invoice Counts — Up to 18%

Most states won't enforce a rate a client never signed. South Dakota is different. Under SDCL § 54-3-5, the statutory interest on money after it becomes due doesn't apply if a written express contract sets a different rate or if the interest rate is clearly shown on the bill, statement or invoice — and a rate shown that way may not exceed 18%. So a clear line such as "Balances unpaid after 30 days accrue interest at 1.5% per month (18% per year)" printed on every invoice does real work in South Dakota, even for clients who never signed a formal agreement.

No Rate Anywhere? The 15% Category F Rate

If neither the contract nor the invoice states a rate, § 54-3-5 applies the Category F rate from the state's official rate table in § 54-3-16 — currently 15% a year — to money after it becomes due on a written instrument, money lent, and money due on settlement of an account. That's one of the highest statutory fallbacks in the country.

Small Claims: $12,000

South Dakota's small claims procedure covers claims of $12,000 or less (SDCL § 16-12C-13), designed so you can file and handle the claim yourself. Confirm the current limit and fee with the clerk, since both change from time to time.

Six Years to Sue

Contract actions must be brought within 6 years (SDCL § 15-2-13).

Setting Up Enforceable Late Fees in South Dakota

  • Print the rate on every invoice — in South Dakota it's enforceable up to 18% even without a signed clause (§ 54-3-5).
  • Put it in the contract too if you want a rate above 18% or a flat late fee; § 54-3-1.1 has no cap on a written agreement.
  • Use a grace period and a monthly or annual rate, not a daily one — a penalty can still be challenged.
  • Keep invoices dated, since the 15% default runs from when the money became due.

Collecting in South Dakota

  • Small claims: up to $12,000 (SDCL § 16-12C-13)
  • Rate shown only on the invoice: enforceable, capped at 18% (§ 54-3-5)
  • No agreed or invoiced rate: Category F rate, 15% a year (§§ 54-3-5, 54-3-16)
  • Statute of limitations: 6 years on a contract (§ 15-2-13)

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a limit on late fees in South Dakota? +

Not for a rate agreed in writing — SDCL § 54-3-1.1 removes usury limits on written agreements. A rate shown only on a bill, statement or invoice is enforceable but capped at 18% (§ 54-3-5). Courts can still refuse a charge that works as a penalty.

Can I charge interest just by putting it on my invoice in South Dakota? +

Yes. Under SDCL § 54-3-5, a rate clearly shown on the bill, statement or invoice replaces the statutory rate, up to 18% a year.

What interest applies in South Dakota if nothing states a rate? +

The Category F rate from SDCL § 54-3-16, currently 15% a year, on money after it becomes due.

What is the small claims limit in South Dakota? +

$12,000 or less (SDCL § 16-12C-13). Check the current limit and fee with the clerk of court.
