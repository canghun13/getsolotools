Late Fee Laws for Freelancers in Iowa

July 27, 2026 · 7 min read

Iowa's general usury cap is one of the most complicated in the country — a rate that floats monthly based on Treasury yields — but a business-purpose exemption likely lets most freelance invoices skip it entirely. Here's what Iowa freelancers need to know about setting and enforcing late fees.

The Problem: Iowa's Usury Cap Is a Moving Target

Most states in this series set a flat percentage as their usury ceiling. Iowa does something unusual: under Iowa Code §535.2(3), the general maximum lawful rate of interest for written agreements floats every month, set at two percentage points above the monthly average 10-year Treasury yield and published by the Superintendent of Banking by the 20th of the preceding month. That rate has often landed in the mid-single digits — well under the 18% (1.5%/month) that most freelancers charge nationally — which would make a standard late fee clause technically unenforceable under the general rule.

The good news: Iowa Code §535.2(2) lists several categories of transactions exempt from this floating cap entirely, including credit extended for a business purpose — defined broadly as "a commercial, service, or industrial enterprise carried on for profit." A freelance invoice billed to a business client for services rendered fits squarely within that definition. In practice, this means the standard 1.5%/month rate is very likely enforceable for Iowa freelancers billing business clients, as long as the rate is clearly stated in a written agreement — the exemption applies to the transaction's purpose, not to freelancers as a category, so document the business relationship clearly.

Iowa's $6,500 Small Claims Limit and Flat $95 Filing Fee

Iowa's Small Claims Docket, part of the District Court under Iowa Code Chapter 631, hears disputes up to $6,500 — a specific, slightly unusual figure compared to the round numbers ($5,000, $10,000) used in most neighboring states. Iowa also stands out procedurally: filing costs a flat $95 statewide under Iowa Code §631.6, regardless of county or claim size, unlike the sliding, county-dependent fee schedules common elsewhere in this series. No attorney is required to file, and the clerk handles service by certified mail at no extra charge.

Setting Up Enforceable Late Fees in Iowa

  • Write your rate into every contract and identify the engagement as a business-to-business arrangement — this is what puts you under Iowa's business-purpose exemption rather than the floating general usury cap.
  • State the rate on the invoice itself, not just in the original agreement, so it's visible when payment becomes overdue.
  • Know your court options. For invoices up to $6,500, Iowa's Small Claims Docket is the fastest path, with a flat $95 filing fee; above that, plan for the regular District Court civil docket.
  • File within 10 years for written contracts (Iowa Code §614.1(5)) — a generous window, though oral agreements carry only a 5-year deadline.

Iowa's usury rate is genuinely unusual — it changes every month. Unlike states with a fixed percentage cap, Iowa's Superintendent of Banking recalculates the general maximum lawful rate monthly based on Treasury yields. Freelancers relying on the general (non-business-purpose) rate should treat any specific percentage as a snapshot rather than a fixed number — another reason documenting your invoice as a business-purpose transaction, where the floating cap doesn't apply at all, is the more reliable path.

Collecting in Iowa

  • Iowa Small Claims Docket: up to $6,500, flat $95 filing fee
  • Iowa District Court (regular civil docket): for claims above $6,500
  • Written contract statute of limitations: 10 years (Iowa Code §614.1(5)); oral contracts: 5 years
  • Post-judgment interest: roughly 6.5–7% per year under Iowa Code §535.3
