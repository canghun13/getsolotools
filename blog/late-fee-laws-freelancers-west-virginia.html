The Problem: WV's Usury Cap Looks Low — Until You Read the Exemption

Under W. Va. Code §47-6-5, the legal rate of interest is 6% per year when a contract doesn't specify a rate, and written agreements are generally capped at 8% per year — well below the 18% (1.5%/month) that most freelancers standardize on nationally. Taken at face value, that would make West Virginia one of the more restrictive states in this series. But §47-6-11 carves out installment sales and loans made for business purposes from the usury limits entirely. A freelance invoice billed to a business client for services rendered is, in substance, a business-purpose extension of credit — which means the 6%/8% ceiling likely doesn't apply to it at all, similar to the "no cap once documented" pattern seen in Oklahoma and several other states in this series.

The practical takeaway: get your rate in writing, and if you're billing a company (not an individual consumer), you're very likely covered by the business-purpose exemption. If your engagement is genuinely with an individual for personal (non-business) purposes, staying at or below 8% per year is the more conservative path until you've confirmed your situation with counsel.

West Virginia's Small Claims Limit Just Doubled — Many Sites Haven't Caught Up

West Virginia's Magistrate Court hears civil claims up to $20,000 — but that's only true as of July 2025, when HB 2761 doubled the prior $10,000 ceiling. A lot of older small-claims guides you'll find online (and even some still being republished) cite the outdated $10,000 figure, so don't be surprised if you see conflicting numbers elsewhere. Filing fees are modest, attorneys are permitted but not required, and corporate parties can now appear pro se through an agent under the same bill. Appeals go to Circuit Court for a fresh trial (de novo) within 20 days.

Setting Up Enforceable Late Fees in West Virginia

  • Put your rate in writing and identify the client as a business entity where applicable — this is what puts you under the business-purpose exemption in §47-6-11 rather than the general 6%/8% usury ceiling.
  • State the rate on the invoice itself, not just in the master agreement, so it's visible the moment payment becomes overdue.
  • Know your court options. For invoices up to $20,000, Magistrate Court is fast and inexpensive; above that, Circuit Court is the venue.
  • File within 10 years for written contracts (W. Va. Code §55-2-6) — one of the longest windows in the nation — though oral agreements carry only a 5-year deadline, so get it in writing.

Corporate clients change your legal footing. If your client is an LLC, corporation, or other registered business entity, West Virginia's business-purpose exemption (§47-6-11) generally takes your invoice out of the usury analysis altogether. If you're ever unsure whether a specific client relationship qualifies, a standard 1.5%/month clause stated clearly in a signed agreement with a business client is the safest way to rely on this exemption in practice.

Collecting in West Virginia

West Virginia's Magistrate Court, present in all 55 counties, hears civil disputes up to $20,000 (W. Va. Code §50-2-1, as amended by HB 2761 in 2025).

  • West Virginia Magistrate Court: up to $20,000, filing fees roughly $30–$75
  • West Virginia Circuit Court: for claims above $20,000
  • Written contract statute of limitations: 10 years (W. Va. Code §55-2-6); oral contracts: 5 years
  • Magistrate Court appeal: de novo trial in Circuit Court within 20 days of judgment
