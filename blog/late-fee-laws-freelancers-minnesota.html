Late Fee Laws for Freelancers in Minnesota

July 16, 2026 · Updated Nov 2, 2026 · 7 min read

Minnesota's usury law sets a lower ceiling than most states for a written contract — 8% annually — but its Conciliation Court (the state's version of small claims court) allows claims up to $15,000, one of the higher limits in the country. Freelancers working in Minneapolis also have a city ordinance that requires a written contract and on-time payment. Here's what Minnesota freelancers need to know about late fees, the state's interest rate rules, Minneapolis's rules, and collecting through Conciliation Court.

Minnesota's Interest Rate Rules

Minnesota's default legal interest rate is 6% per year (Minn. Stat. § 334.01), and the same statute caps a written contract rate at 8% per year for most agreements — lower than the 12-24% range many other states allow. That said, Minnesota carves out real exceptions for business transactions: § 334.011 permits business-purpose contracts under $100,000 to charge a variable commercial rate tied to the 90-day commercial paper discount rate plus 4.5 percentage points. Note what that exception does and doesn't do: it lifts the ceiling to roughly 4.5 points above a short-term market rate, not to 18%.

Many freelancers still copy the nationwide habit of 1.5% per month (18% annually) onto Minnesota invoices, but Minnesota is one of the few states where that number is genuinely exposed: § 334.01 covers forbearing "money, goods, or things in action," and a usurious contract can cost the lender the interest entirely. The conservative written rate is 8% per year (about 0.66% per month). If you want more leverage than that, add a modest one-time flat late charge stated in the contract, which compensates for collection costs rather than the use of money — see charging a flat late fee and interest together. Either way, put the terms in a written contract before work begins.

A Generous Small Claims Limit

Minnesota's Conciliation Court allows claims up to $15,000 — one of the higher limits in the country (only a few states, such as Texas at $20,000 and Tennessee and Delaware at $25,000, go higher), well above the $5,000-$10,000 range typical in most states. That means the vast majority of freelance invoice disputes, even sizable ones, can be handled without an attorney or the cost of formal civil court.

Minneapolis Freelance Worker Protections

Minnesota has no statewide freelancer protection law, but Minneapolis does. Since January 1, 2021, the city's freelance worker ordinance has covered independent contractors performing services in Minneapolis when the work is worth $600 or more in a calendar year (all contracts between the parties combined) or $200 or more within seven consecutive days. Attorneys, licensed medical professionals and certain commissioned sales representatives are excluded.

  • Written contract. Names and addresses, an itemization of the services, the rate and method of payment, and the payment date or how it will be set — signed by the freelancer.
  • Payment deadline. By the contract date, or within 30 days after the work is completed if the contract doesn't set one.
  • No pay-cut squeeze. The hiring party can't demand that you accept less as a condition of being paid on time once work has started.
  • Remedies. The unpaid amount plus liquidated damages of up to double that amount, along with city fines that rise for repeat violations. Failing to provide a written contract after you request one can draw a fine of up to $250.

If you work for clients from a Minneapolis office or on-site in the city, cite the ordinance in your contract and in your first overdue reminder. For work performed elsewhere in Minnesota, your contract is your main enforcement tool.

Setting Up Enforceable Late Fees in Minnesota

  • Stay at or under 8% per year for interest unless an attorney confirms an exception fits your client — the business-purpose exception in § 334.011 applies only to business transactions and still sits well below 18%.
  • Put the rate in writing before work begins. "Invoices unpaid after 30 days accrue simple interest at 8% per year on the outstanding balance" is clear and stays within the general cap.
  • Repeat your terms on every invoice so the client sees them without digging through the original agreement.
  • Send a written reminder before fees kick in — this creates a clean paper trail if the dispute ever reaches Conciliation Court.

Collecting in Minnesota

Minnesota's Conciliation Court — the state's small claims court — encourages mediation before trial, which resolves many disputes without a formal hearing. No attorney is required, and the process is designed for self-represented filers.

  • Minnesota Conciliation Court: up to $15,000 — no attorney required, mediation encouraged
  • Minnesota District Court: for claims above $15,000
  • No rate in the contract: 6% per year on money due (Minn. Stat. § 334.01)
  • Judgment interest (Minn. Stat. § 549.09): for judgments of $50,000 or less, a rate the state court administrator sets each year from the one-year Treasury yield; 10% per year for most judgments over $50,000

How does Minnesota's small claims limit compare to Wisconsin? +

Minnesota's Conciliation Court allows claims up to $15,000, 50% higher than neighboring Wisconsin's $10,000 small claims limit. Minnesota's interest rate rules are more layered, though — an 8% usury cap applies to most written contracts, with separate exceptions for business-purpose transactions.
