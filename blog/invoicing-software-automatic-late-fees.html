Which Invoicing Tools Actually Auto-Apply Late Fees (And What Rate to Set in Your State)

August 3, 2026 · 9 min read

"Set it and forget it" is the promise of automatic late fees — turn on a rule once, and your invoicing tool charges overdue clients without you sending another awkward email. The problem is that not every invoicing tool actually does this, and the ones that do won't tell you if the rate you picked happens to be illegal in your state. We fact-checked ten invoicing tools directly against their own documentation and support communities, then cross-referenced the rate question against our own state-by-state late fee law research.

The Problem: "Automatic" Doesn't Mean What You Think It Means

Every invoicing tool markets some version of "automatic late fees" or "late payment reminders" on its pricing page, and it's easy to assume they all do the same thing. They don't. Some tools calculate the fee, generate a new invoice line, and send it without you lifting a finger. Others only send an email reminder — the actual fee still has to be typed in by hand. A few well-known tools, including one of the biggest names in small-business accounting, don't have a native late-fee feature at all, at any price tier, and most of their users don't find that out until they go looking for the setting and can't find it.

The second, less obvious problem: even the tools that automate the calculation don't check whether your chosen rate is legal where your client is based. A default of 1.5% per month (18% annually) is the industry-standard freelancer rate, but it isn't universally enforceable — a few states cap written-contract interest below that, and several others only permit it because the invoice counts as a business-to-business transaction rather than a personal loan. Automating an illegal rate doesn't make it enforceable; it just makes it easier to apply consistently.

The Surprising Gap: Xero Has No Native Late-Fee Feature

If you use Xero, this is worth knowing before you go looking for a setting that doesn't exist: Xero has never built a native way to calculate or apply late fees or interest on overdue invoices, at any subscription tier. The feature request has sat open on Xero's own product ideas forum for more than a decade, with commenters repeatedly pointing out that direct competitors like FreshBooks and QuickBooks have had this built in for years. The only way to automate it on Xero is to install a third-party app from the Xero App Store — Paidnice is the most commonly used one — which connects to your account and applies fee rules on top of Xero's native invoicing. Without an add-on, the accepted workaround is to manually create a separate invoice referencing the original one, which is exactly the manual process automatic late fees are supposed to eliminate.

The Other Gap: "Zoho Invoice" Isn't the Same Product as "Zoho Books"

Zoho's naming causes genuine confusion here. Zoho Invoice — the free, standalone product most freelancers mean when they say "I use Zoho" — only supports late fees as a manual line item. Zoho Books and Zoho Billing, Zoho's paid accounting and subscription-billing products, do have a configurable automatic late-fee feature (percentage or fixed amount, with a grace period and customer exclusions). If you're on the free Zoho Invoice plan and assumed you had automatic late fees because you read about "Zoho's late fee feature" somewhere, you're likely looking at documentation for a different, paid Zoho product.

Setting This Up Without Creating a Legal Problem

  • Put the rate in your contract or invoice terms before you turn on automation. HoneyBook actually enforces this — it won't let you enable late fees without a contract clause referencing them first — but every tool on this list needs the same paper trail to make the fee enforceable, whether or not the software requires it.
  • Check your state's cap before setting an account-wide default, especially if most of your clients are concentrated in one state. See the table above and our full 34-state comparison.
  • Use a grace period. Every tool that supports automatic late fees lets you set a delay (commonly 7–14 days) before the fee applies — this avoids penalizing a client who paid the same day it became overdue but before the automation ran.
  • Don't expect retroactive application. QuickBooks Online, for example, only applies its automatic fee going forward from when you enable the setting — invoices already overdue won't get the fee unless you add it manually.

If a client still doesn't pay after the automated fee kicks in, the software's job is done — what comes next is a judgment call about reminders, escalation, and when to consider small claims court, which we cover in How to Handle Late Payments as a Freelancer. Frequently Asked Questions

Which invoicing tools automatically apply late fees to overdue invoices? +

FreshBooks, Bonsai, HoneyBook, Invoice Ninja, and QuickBooks Online all support genuinely automatic late fees — you set a percentage or flat rate and a grace period once, and the tool applies it without you touching the invoice again. Zoho Invoice, Wave, Stripe Invoicing, and PayPal do not; you'd need to add the fee manually as a line item or a separate invoice.

Does Xero automatically apply late fees? +

No — and this surprises a lot of Xero users. Xero has no native late-fee feature at any plan tier; the request has been open on Xero's own product ideas board for over a decade with no committed timeline. To automate late fees on Xero invoices you need a third-party app from the Xero App Store, such as Paidnice. Without one, adding a late fee in Xero means manually creating a separate invoice for the interest and referencing the original.

Is a 1.5% monthly late fee legal in every state? +

No. Most states allow it, but a handful cap written-contract interest below the common 1.5%/month (18% annual) standard — Arkansas caps it at 17% annually (about 1.4%/month) under its state constitution, for example. Several other states allow it but only because the invoice qualifies as a business-purpose transaction rather than a personal loan. Check the specific state law before turning on an automatic percentage in any invoicing tool.
