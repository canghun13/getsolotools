Late Fee Laws for Freelancers & Small Businesses in Vermont

November 23, 2026 · 7 min read

Vermont's legal rate of interest is 12% a year — among the highest statutory rates in the country — and the same 12% applies to judgments. That's good news when a contract is silent, but it also works as a ceiling for many transactions, with exceptions for corporations and business financing. Here's what Vermont freelancers need to know.

Vermont's 12% Legal Rate

Under 9 V.S.A. § 41a(a), the rate of interest is 12% a year except as specifically provided by law. With no agreed rate, that's what an unpaid balance earns — roughly double the default in many states. Judgments also carry 12% a year (12 V.S.A. § 2903).

When a Client Can Agree to More

Section 41a(b) sets higher limits for particular kinds of credit (retail installment contracts, charge agreements and so on), and 9 V.S.A. § 46 lets the parties agree to a rate above § 41a for obligations of corporations and for loans that finance an income-producing business or activity. Overcharging is serious in Vermont: a knowing violation can cost the lender all interest and half the principal (§ 50(b)).

Practical rule: 1% a month (12% a year) is the safe written rate for any client. If your client is a corporation, a higher agreed rate is allowed by § 46, but most Vermont freelancers don't need it — 12% already beats the national norm for a statutory fallback.

A reasonable delinquency charge that's clearly stated in the contract isn't treated as interest (§ 44), so a modest one-time late fee can sit alongside the 12%.

Small Claims: $10,000

Vermont's small claims procedure covers claims of $10,000 or less (12 V.S.A. § 5531), and a larger claim can't be split to fit. For claims over $3,500, the defendant can ask for a specially assigned judicial officer.

Six Years to Sue

Contract claims must be filed within 6 years (12 V.S.A. § 511).

Setting Up Enforceable Late Fees in Vermont

  • Write 1% a month (12% a year) — it matches Vermont's legal rate and works for any client.
  • Add a modest one-time delinquency charge in the contract if you want one; § 44 doesn't count it as interest.
  • Only go above 12% with corporate clients or genuine business financing (§ 46).
  • Put the rate on every invoice and keep the signed agreement.

Collecting in Vermont

  • Small claims: up to $10,000 (12 V.S.A. § 5531)
  • Legal rate with no agreement: 12% a year (9 V.S.A. § 41a)
  • Judgment interest: 12% a year (12 V.S.A. § 2903)
  • Statute of limitations: 6 years on a contract (12 V.S.A. § 511)

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the legal interest rate in Vermont? +

12% a year under 9 V.S.A. § 41a(a), except as otherwise provided by law. Judgments also carry 12% (12 V.S.A. § 2903).

Can I charge more than 12% a year on a Vermont invoice? +

Only in limited cases. 9 V.S.A. § 46 allows a higher agreed rate for obligations of corporations and for loans financing an income-producing business. For other clients, 12% a year (1% a month) is the safe line.

What is the small claims limit in Vermont? +

$10,000 (12 V.S.A. § 5531). Claims can't be split to fit under the limit.

How long do I have to sue over an unpaid invoice in Vermont? +

Six years on a contract claim (12 V.S.A. § 511).
