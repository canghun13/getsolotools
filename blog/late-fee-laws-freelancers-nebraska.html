Late Fee Laws for Freelancers & Small Businesses in Nebraska

October 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Nebraska is a state where the contract does almost all the work. The fallback interest rate is just 6% a year, the 16% ceiling on agreed rates comes with exemptions most guides skip, and small claims went up to $7,500 in July 2025. Here's what Nebraska freelancers and small businesses need to know.

No Rate in the Contract? Nebraska's Default Is Only 6%

Nebraska's legal rate under Neb. Rev. Stat. § 45-102 is 6% a year. That's the number a court falls back on when your contract is silent — and it's low. Kansas gives 10%, Missouri 9%. On a $5,000 invoice that's 90 days late, 6% a year is about $74; 1.25% a month is about $188. In Nebraska more than most states, the rate you write into the contract is the rate you'll actually be able to collect.

The 16% Cap — and Who It Doesn't Cover

Nebraska's general ceiling on an agreed rate is 16% a year (§ 45-101.03). But § 45-101.04 exempts a long list of transactions, including:

  • Loans to corporations, partnerships and trusts
  • Business or agricultural purpose loans
  • Principal amounts over $25,000
  • Open credit accounts

An invoice to an incorporated business client is likely outside the cap. But these exemptions are written for lending, and a late charge on a service invoice isn't a classic loan — whether a given client and invoice fit an exemption is the kind of question that only gets settled in a dispute.

The rate that holds under every reading: 1.25% a month (15% a year). It sits under the 16% ceiling even if the cap applies. The national-standard 1.5% a month (18% a year) is above 16%, so reserve it for clients that are clearly entities — an LLC, a corporation — and keep individuals at 1.25% or lower.

Whatever rate you choose, state it monthly or annually, never per day — see is a daily late fee legal on an invoice.

Nebraska Small Claims Is $7,500 — Since July 2025

Nebraska adjusts its small claims ceiling on a schedule. Under § 25-2802, it was $6,000 from July 1, 2024 through June 30, 2025, and is $7,500 from July 1, 2025. Older guides may show $6,000 or less.

  • No lawyers. Under § 25-2803, no party may be represented by an attorney in small claims. An LLC is represented by a member, manager or employee.
  • Filing limits. No party may file more than two claims in a calendar week or ten in a calendar year — worth knowing if you have several late payers at once.
  • Only your own claims. Assigned claims can't be filed in small claims.

Above $7,500, Nebraska's county courts hear civil cases up to $70,000. Whether it's worth your hours is a separate question — is it worth suing a client over an unpaid invoice works through the arithmetic.

Setting Up Enforceable Late Fees in Nebraska

  • Always write a rate in — the 6% default is too low to be a real incentive.
  • Use 1.25% a month by default, 1.5% only for clearly incorporated clients.
  • Put the rate and due date on every invoice, not only in the original agreement.
  • Get the engagement in writing — five years to sue on a written contract versus four on an oral one.

Collecting in Nebraska

  • Small claims: up to $7,500 from July 1, 2025 (§ 25-2802); no attorneys; two claims a week, ten a year (§ 25-2803)
  • County court: civil cases up to $70,000 (July 2025 – June 2030)
  • Default interest with no agreed rate: 6% a year (§ 45-102)
  • Statute of limitations: 5 years for written contracts, 4 years for oral contracts
