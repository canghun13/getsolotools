Late Fee Laws for Freelancers & Small Businesses in Montana

October 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Montana gives an unpaid invoice 10% a year from the day it's due even without a late fee clause, and eight years to sue on a written contract. In exchange, it caps agreed rates at the greater of 15% or prime plus 6 — so the national-standard 1.5% a month doesn't quite fit. Here's what Montana freelancers and small businesses need to know.

No Rate in the Contract? Montana Gives You 10% From the Due Date

Under Mont. Code Ann. § 31-1-106, interest is payable at 10% a year after money becomes due on written instruments and accounts stated — which is what an accepted invoice becomes. That's at the high end of statutory defaults: Colorado gives 8%, Idaho 12%, many states 5–6%. It runs from the due date, not from the day you file suit, and a written contract rate replaces it.

The Agreed-Rate Ceiling: 15% or Prime + 6

Mont. Code Ann. § 31-1-107 lets parties agree in writing to any rate up to the greater of 15% a year or 6 percentage points above the prime rate, measured from the Federal Reserve's published prime three business days before the agreement is signed. Whenever prime is below 9%, prime + 6 comes in under 15% — so in practice 15% is the operative ceiling.

Why the calculator defaults to 1.25% a month. The statute is written for loans, and whether it caps a late charge on a service invoice hasn't been settled. 1.25% a month is exactly 15% a year — inside the ceiling under any reading. The national-standard 1.5% a month (18%) only fits when prime is at 12% or higher — far above where it has been in recent decades.

Whatever rate you write, state it monthly or annually — a fee written per day compounds past any ceiling. See is a daily late fee legal on an invoice.

Montana Small Claims: $7,000

Montana's small claims court hears claims up to $7,000, exclusive of costs (Mont. Code Ann. § 25-35-502), as long as the client can be served in the county where you file. Lawyers generally don't represent parties at the hearing, and a client that's an LLC or corporation appears through an officer or employee. Above $7,000 you're in regular civil court. Is it worth suing a client over an unpaid invoice runs that trade-off against your own time.

Eight Years on a Written Contract

Mont. Code Ann. § 27-2-202 gives 8 years to sue on a written contract and 5 years on an oral contract or an account. Both are long by national standards, but the three-year gap is a good reason to get even small engagements confirmed in writing — an email thread where the client accepts your quote and terms counts.

Setting Up Enforceable Late Fees in Montana

  • Write 1.25% a month (15% a year) — the rate that fits under § 31-1-107 regardless of prime.
  • Get the engagement in writing — 8 years to sue instead of 5, and a written rate replaces the 10% default.
  • Put the rate and due date on every invoice, so an accepted invoice is clearly an account stated.
  • Check where the client can be served before choosing small claims — it has to be the county where you file.

Collecting in Montana

  • Small claims: up to $7,000, exclusive of costs (§ 25-35-502)
  • Default interest with no agreed rate: 10% a year after money becomes due (§ 31-1-106)
  • Agreed-rate ceiling: greater of 15% a year or prime + 6 points (§ 31-1-107)
  • Statute of limitations: 8 years for written contracts, 5 years for oral contracts and accounts (§ 27-2-202)
