Freelance Contract Email Template

A contract protects both you and the client — it defines what you're delivering, what you're being paid, and what happens if things don't go as planned. Sending it with a clear, professional email makes it feel like a normal part of doing business, not an adversarial formality.

When to send Send the contract immediately after the welcome email, or alongside it. Never start work before the contract is signed and any required deposit is received.

The Template

Email Template
Subject: Contract for [Project Name] - Please Review and Sign
Hi [Client Name], As discussed, please find the project contract attached for your review. The contract covers: - Project scope and deliverables - Timeline and milestones - Payment terms and schedule - Revision policy - Intellectual property and ownership Please read through it and let me know if you have any questions or if anything needs clarification. Once you're comfortable with the terms, please sign and return it. I'll get started as soon as I receive the signed contract and the initial payment of $[deposit amount]. Looking forward to working together. Best, [Your Name]

Five More Ways to Send a Contract by Email

The template above is for a first contract with a new client. These cover the other moments a contract email has to handle: a repeat client on the same terms, a link from an e-signature tool, a contract that isn't ready yet, a nudge when it hasn't come back, and a revised version after negotiation.

Repeat client — same terms as last time
Subject: Contract for [Project Name] – Same Terms as [Previous Project]
Hi [Client Name], Attached is the contract for [project name]. It uses the same terms as our agreement for [previous project] — the only changes are the scope, timeline and fee in Sections [X] and [Y]: - Scope: [one-line summary] - Timeline: [start date] to [end date] - Fee: $[amount], [payment schedule] If those look right, please sign and return it, and I'll schedule the kickoff for [date]. Best, [Your Name]
Sending through DocuSign, Dropbox Sign or another e-signature tool
Subject: Please Sign: [Project Name] Agreement (via [DocuSign])
Hi [Client Name], You'll receive a separate email from [DocuSign] titled "[envelope title]" with the agreement for [project name]. It takes about two minutes to review and sign — no printing needed. Before you sign, the three things worth checking are: 1. The scope in Section [X] matches what we discussed on [date] 2. The payment schedule: [deposit] on signing, [balance] on [milestone] 3. The start date of [date] If the signing email doesn't arrive within the hour, check your spam folder or let me know and I'll resend it. Thanks, [Your Name]
The contract is being prepared — status update
Subject: [Project Name] Agreement – On Its Way by [Date]
Hi [Client Name], Quick update: I've started preparing the agreement for [project name] and will send it by [date]. It will cover the scope we agreed on [date], the timeline, and the payment schedule ([deposit]% to start, the balance on delivery). If anything has changed on your side — scope, start date, or who will sign for [Company] — let me know before then so the first draft is the final one. Best, [Your Name]
Follow-up when the contract hasn't been signed
Subject: Following Up: [Project Name] Agreement Sent [Date]
Hi [Client Name], I'm following up on the agreement for [project name] I sent on [date]. I'm holding [start date] for this project, and I'll need the signed agreement and deposit by [date] to keep that slot. If there's a term you'd like to discuss, or if someone else at [Company] needs to review it, just let me know who and I'll send it to them directly. Best, [Your Name]
Sending a revised contract after negotiation
Subject: Revised Agreement: [Project Name] – Changes from Our Call
Hi [Client Name], Thanks for going through the agreement with me on [date]. Attached is the revised version with the changes we discussed: - Section [X]: [change, e.g. payment terms moved from Net 15 to Net 30] - Section [Y]: [change, e.g. revision rounds increased from two to three] - Section [Z]: [change] Everything else is unchanged from the version I sent on [date]. If this matches your understanding, please sign and return it and we'll start on [date]. Best, [Your Name]

What to Say When Sending a Contract by Email

Whatever the situation, a contract email does five jobs. Skip one and you usually get a delay:

  • Name the document and the project in the subject line, so it can be found in an inbox later.
  • Summarize the three terms people actually check — scope, timeline and payment — so the reader knows what they're agreeing to before opening the file.
  • Say how to sign (attached PDF, e-signature link, or print and scan) and who needs to sign if it isn't the person you're emailing.
  • Tie the start date to the signature and deposit, in one plain sentence.
  • Invite questions in writing, so any negotiated change ends up in a revised contract rather than a phone call nobody remembers.

Tips for Getting This Right

  • List what the contract covers in the email so the client knows what to look for before they open it.
  • Use an e-signature tool (DocuSign, HelloSign, PandaDoc) to reduce the friction of printing, signing, and scanning.
  • Tie the project start explicitly to receipt of the signed contract and deposit — state this clearly in the email.
  • If the client wants to negotiate terms, handle it in writing so you have a clear record of what was agreed.
  • Keep your contract terms in a template so you can send it quickly without drafting from scratch each time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Starting work before the contract is signed — verbal agreements are difficult to enforce. No exceptions.
  • Using a contract template that's too generic — your contract should specifically address your type of work and the most common disputes in your industry.
  • Not requiring a deposit — a signed contract with no deposit still leaves you exposed. Deposits signal commitment from the client.
  • Caving to pressure to 'start now and sign later' — this almost always ends badly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer to create a freelance contract?

Not necessarily. There are many solid freelance contract templates available, and a well-written template often covers the essentials. For complex projects or high-value contracts, a one-time legal review is worth the cost.

What if the client refuses to sign a contract?

That's a serious red flag. A legitimate client who respects you as a professional will have no objection to a standard contract. Refusing to sign suggests they want to avoid accountability. Walk away.

Can I use an e-signature?

Yes — e-signatures (DocuSign, HelloSign, Adobe Sign) are legally binding in most jurisdictions and far more convenient than physical signatures. Most clients prefer them.

What's the most important clause in a freelance contract?

The scope of work and the payment terms are the two most critical. Scope defines what you're building so there's no argument later. Payment terms define when and how you get paid so there's no ambiguity at invoice time.

What should I say when sending a contract by email?

Name the project, summarize the scope, timeline and payment terms in a few lines, explain how to sign, and state that work starts once the signed contract and deposit arrive. Invite questions in writing so changes end up in a revised version.

How do I follow up on an unsigned contract?

Wait two or three business days, then send a short follow-up that references the date you sent it, the start date you're holding, and the deadline for keeping that slot. Offer to send it directly to whoever else needs to review it.
